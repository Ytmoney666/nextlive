WASHINGTON 1986 Cold Case Solved After 32 Years — Justice for Michella Welch

On Wednesday, March 26, 1986, 12-year-old Michella Welch was looking after her two younger sisters at Puget Park in north Tacoma, Washington. She rode her bike home to pick up their lunch and brought it back to the park. Around 12:30, her sisters walked to a nearby business to use the restroom. When they returned at about 1 p.m., Michella was gone. Her bike and the lunch were still there. That night, a search dog found her in the wooded gulch next to the park. Her killer left DNA behind, but in 1986 no one could use it. For decades, police believed the same man had also killed 13-year-old Jennifer Bastian in Point Defiance Park that August. In 2013 a long-untested piece of evidence proved there were two killers. Then a cold case detective, a list of 2,300 names, genetic genealogy built from strangers' family trees, and one discarded restaurant napkin finally gave Michella's family an answer, 32 years after that day at the park.

In this documentary: who Michella was, the day she disappeared, the two-killer mistake that cost decades, the genetic genealogy breakthrough, and the court case that followed.
