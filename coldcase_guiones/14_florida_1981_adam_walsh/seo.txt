FLORIDA 1981 Cold Case — Police Named Adam Walsh's Killer After 27 Years

On Monday, July 27, 1981, six-year-old Adam Walsh stopped to watch older boys play an Atari video game in the toy department of a Sears store at the Hollywood Mall in Hollywood, Florida. His mother, Revé, stepped away to shop nearby for just a few minutes. When she came back, Adam was gone. Two weeks later, on August 10, 1981, fishermen in a canal beside the Florida Turnpike near Vero Beach, about 120 miles away, made the discovery that told the Walsh family he had been murdered. Then came a prison confession, a recantation, blood on a car's carpet that the science of 1983 could not identify, and a devastating failure: by 1994, when DNA testing was finally possible, the car and the carpet had vanished from police custody. For 27 years, the case stayed open, while John and Revé Walsh turned their grief into the Missing Children Act, a national center for missing children, America's Most Wanted, the Code Adam alert and the Adam Walsh Act. On December 16, 2008, the Hollywood police chief stood before cameras, apologized, and said a name.

In this documentary: who Adam was, the few minutes inside Sears, the canal near Vero Beach, the confessions and the lost evidence, the detective who reviewed it all again, the 2008 announcement, and the laws and alerts that carry Adam's name today.
