TEXAS 1995 Cold Case Solved After 26 Years — Justice for Mary Catherine Edwards

On Saturday, January 14, 1995, a family in Beaumont, Texas, kept calling a phone that nobody answered. It belonged to Mary Catherine Edwards, 31, an elementary school teacher at Price Elementary who called her students "her kids." When her family went to her townhome, they found her in an upstairs bathroom. She had been sexually assaulted and killed, and her hands were held behind her back by police-grade Smith & Wesson handcuffs. There was no sign of forced entry. The handcuffs made investigators fear the killer could be one of their own. The DNA he left behind matched no one in the national database, and for 26 years the case stayed cold. In April 2020, during the pandemic, the evidence went to a lab north of Houston that could read more than half a million genetic markers. A second cousin in Louisiana, a family tree and a bag of trash in Ohio finally produced a name, and a connection no one had imagined: Mary Catherine had once been a bridesmaid at his wedding.
