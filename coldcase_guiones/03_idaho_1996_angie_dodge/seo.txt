IDAHO 1996 Cold Case Solved After 23 Years — Justice for Angie Dodge

On the morning of June 13, 1996, coworkers worried that 18-year-old Angie Dodge had not come to work went to her first apartment, at 444 I Street in Idaho Falls, Idaho. They found her dead. She had been raped and stabbed, and her attacker had left his DNA at the scene. Police soon focused on a 20-year-old named Christopher Tapp. After repeated interrogations, five polygraph tests and the threat of the death penalty, he confessed. His DNA did not match. A jury convicted him anyway in 1998. For two decades, the DNA from Angie's apartment belonged to no one. And the person who fought hardest to find its owner was Angie's own mother, Carol Dodge, who came to believe that the man in prison was innocent. In 2018 she pushed for genetic genealogy on a badly degraded sample. What it revealed, 23 years after Angie's murder, was something no one in Idaho Falls had imagined, and it had been close by all along.

In this documentary: who Angie was, the false confession that sent an innocent man to prison, a mother's fight for the truth, the genetic genealogy breakthrough, and the court case that followed.
