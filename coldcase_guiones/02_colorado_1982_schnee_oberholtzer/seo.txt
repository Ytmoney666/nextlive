COLORADO 1982 Cold Case Solved After 39 Years — Justice for Annette and Bobbie Jo

On January 6, 1982, in the middle of a snowstorm, two young women who had never met hitchhiked out of Breckenridge, Colorado. Annette Kay Schnee, 21, originally from Sioux City, Iowa, was heading back to Blue River. Barbara "Bobbie Jo" Oberholtzer, 29, called her husband to tell him she had a ride home to Alma. The next day, Jeff Oberholtzer found his wife's bloodstained glove and a scarf by the road, and that afternoon Bobbie Jo's body was found in the snow near the top of Hoosier Pass. She had been shot twice. Beside her lay an orange sock that was not hers. That July, a boy fishing with his father found Annette in Sacramento Creek near Fairplay. She was wearing the other orange sock, a Christmas gift from her mother. For years, suspicion fell on the wrong man, until DNA cleared him in the early 1990s. The blood on that glove waited 39 years. Then genetic genealogy and one discarded item led investigators to a man who had crossed paths with rescuers on the very night of the murders.

In this documentary: the lives of Annette and Bobbie Jo, the orange socks that linked two murders, the years of misplaced suspicion, the DNA breakthrough, and the trial.
