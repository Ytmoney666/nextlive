MASSACHUSETTS 1969 Cold Case — DNA Identified Her Killer After 49 Years | Jane Britton

On January 7, 1969, Jane Sanders Britton, a 23-year-old Harvard anthropology graduate student from Needham, Massachusetts, did not show up for the most important exam of her academic life. Just after noon, her boyfriend and a neighbor opened the unlocked door of her fourth-floor apartment at 6 University Road, a few minutes from Harvard Square, and found her dead. Red ochre, a pigment used in ancient burials, had been sprinkled on her body and around the room. The newspapers spoke of a ritual. Police and Harvard anthropologists rejected the idea, but a campus rumor about a professor took hold and lasted for decades. No one was ever charged. Then, in October 2017, the Massachusetts State Police Crime Lab pulled a male-specific Y-STR DNA profile from swabs that had been stored since 1969. In November 2018, after 49 years, the Middlesex District Attorney announced the name of the man the DNA identified, and it was someone nobody in Jane's world had ever suspected.

In this documentary: Jane's life as a Radcliffe graduate and field archaeologist, her last night, the red ochre and the rumor that followed it, the Y-STR DNA breakthrough, the confirmation through a relative found on Ancestry.com, and why the case was closed without a trial.
