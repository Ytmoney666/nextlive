VERMONT 1971 Cold Case — DNA Identified Her Killer After 51 Years | Rita Curran

In the early hours of July 20, 1971, roommates returning to their apartment on Brookes Avenue in Burlington, Vermont, found Rita Curran, 24, a second-grade teacher from Milton, dead on her bedroom floor. She had been strangled. Beside her arm lay a single cigarette butt. For more than five decades the case stayed cold. Rumors grew that Ted Bundy, who was born in Burlington, might have been responsible, but no evidence ever linked him to the crime. The cigarette stayed in storage. In 2014 a DNA profile was developed, and it matched no one. Then a new team of detectives turned to investigative genetic genealogy, a half-brother volunteered his DNA, and a woman who had given an alibi in 1971 finally told detectives a different story. In February 2023, according to Burlington police, DNA identified the man who killed Rita Curran. He had been questioned the very morning her body was found.

In this documentary: who Rita Curran was, the night she died, the 1971 investigation and the alibi that held for five decades, the Ted Bundy rumor, how genetic genealogy and a cigarette butt identified her killer, and what her family said when the answer came.
