MICHIGAN 1969 Cold Case Solved After 35 Years — Justice for Jane Mixer

On the evening of March 20, 1969, Jane Louise Mixer, a 23-year-old first-year law student at the University of Michigan, left campus in Ann Arbor to go home to Muskegon for spring break. She had found her ride the way students did in those years, through a notice on a campus ride board. Her father waited, then drove the streets for hours looking for her. The next morning she was found inside the fence of Denton Cemetery, two miles east of Ypsilanti. For more than three decades her death was folded into the infamous "Michigan Murders", although no one was ever charged with killing her. Then Michigan State Police reopened the case and sent the evidence, kept since 1969, for DNA testing. The national DNA database returned a name no one had ever connected to the case. It also returned a second name: a man who was only four years old the night Jane died. This is the story of a 35-year wait, a conviction, and a question that is still being asked.

In this documentary: who Jane Mixer was, the night of the ride, the decades her case spent inside the Michigan Murders story, the DNA breakthrough, the 2005 trial and the laboratory contamination debate that still surrounds it.
