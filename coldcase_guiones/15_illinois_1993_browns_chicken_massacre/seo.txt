ILLINOIS 1993 Cold Case Solved After 9 Years — Justice for the Brown's Chicken Seven

On Friday, January 8, 1993, at closing time, seven people were working the last shift at a Brown's Chicken and Pasta on Northwest Highway in Palatine, Illinois: owners Richard Ehlenfeldt, 50, and Lynn Ehlenfeldt, 49, and employees Guadalupe Maldonado, 46, Thomas Mennes, 32, Marcus Nellsen, 31, and two Palatine High School students, Michael Castro, 16, and Rico Solis, 17. None of them came home. Hours later, police found all seven inside the restaurant's walk-in cooler and freezer. A task force of more than one hundred people chased thousands of tips. A $100,000 reward went unclaimed. A watchdog report called the police crime lab a "disaster." The building was torn down. But on the first night, a crime scene investigator pulled a half-eaten chicken dinner out of the trash and kept it. It sat in a freezer for more than nine years, waiting for a name. In 2002, one woman finally decided she could not stay silent anymore.

In this documentary: the lives of the seven victims, the night of the murders, the nine-year investigation and its failures, how DNA on a discarded meal was matched, the 2002 tip that broke the case, the 2007 and 2009 trials, and where the case stands today.
