PENNSYLVANIA 1957 Cold Case Identified After 65 Years — The Boy in the Box Finally Has a Name

On Monday, February 25, 1957, in a wooded lot off Susquehanna Road in Fox Chase, then one of the most rural corners of Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, someone found a cardboard box that had once held a J.C. Penney bassinet. Inside, wrapped in a flannel blanket, was a boy of about four years old. He had been beaten, and he was malnourished. No one ever reported him missing. Police printed 400,000 flyers, some mailed out with gas bills, and hundreds of officers searched. Detectives chased a foster home, a psychic's tip and a woman's story about a boy her mother had acquired. Nothing gave him a name. For 65 years he was known only as the Boy in the Box, America's Unknown Child. Then, after two exhumations, a team of scientists and genealogists recovered enough DNA from his remains to find relatives on his mother's side, and detectives searched old birth records with a court order. In December 2022, police stood at a podium and said his name: Joseph Augustus Zarelli. One question is still open: who killed him.

In this documentary: the boy's short life, the 65-year search for his name, the false trails, the DNA and genetic genealogy breakthrough, and why his death remains an open homicide investigation.
