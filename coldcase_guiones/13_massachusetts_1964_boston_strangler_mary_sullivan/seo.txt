MASSACHUSETTS 1964 Cold Case: Killer Identified After 49 Years — Justice for Mary Sullivan

On January 4, 1964, Mary Sullivan, a 19-year-old from Cape Cod, was sexually assaulted and strangled in the apartment at 44A Charles Street, on Boston's Beacon Hill, that she had moved into only days earlier. Her death became the last of the killings attributed to the "Boston Strangler," a series that had terrified the Boston area since June 1962. A man confessed, but his confession was never tested in court, and for decades even Mary's own family doubted it. In 2001, private DNA tests seemed to point away from him. But evidence collected from Mary's apartment in 1964 had been preserved. In 2013, investigators followed a relative, collected a water bottle he threw away, and used a Y-chromosome DNA test to follow a family line. Then came an exhumation, and an answer 49 years after Mary died. This documentary explains exactly what that DNA proved in Mary's case, and what it did not prove about the other women.

In this documentary: who Mary Sullivan was, the fear that gripped Boston in 1962–1964, the confession that never went to trial, the decades of doubt, the familial DNA breakthrough and exhumation, and why the debate over the other killings continues.
