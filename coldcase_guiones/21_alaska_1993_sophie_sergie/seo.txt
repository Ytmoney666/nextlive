ALASKA 1993 Cold Case Solved After 26 Years — Justice for Sophie Sergie

Shortly after midnight on April 26, 1993, Sophie Sergie, a 20-year-old Yup'ik woman from the tiny village of Pitkas Point on Alaska's Yukon River, stepped out of her friend's room in Bartlett Hall at the University of Alaska Fairbanks to smoke a cigarette. Her friend suggested the women's tub room down the hall, out of the cold. Sophie never came back. That afternoon, janitorial staff found her in a bathtub in the second-floor women's bathroom. She had been sexually assaulted, stabbed and shot. The killer left his DNA behind, but in 1993 Alaska could not yet use it, and for years it matched no one in the national database. Her family waited in their village for almost 26 years. Then, in 2018, Alaska State Troopers tried the same genetic genealogy technique that had just cracked the Golden State Killer case. A relative's DNA, shared on a public genealogy website, led investigators thousands of miles away, to a man no one in his new life had ever connected to Fairbanks.

In this documentary: who Sophie was, her last night in Bartlett Hall, the long cold years, the missing and murdered Indigenous women crisis in Alaska, the genetic genealogy breakthrough, the trial, and the sentence.
