CALIFORNIA 1984 Cold Case Solved After 27 Years — Justice for Tina Faelz

On Thursday, April 5, 1984, 14-year-old Tina Faelz, a freshman at Foothill High School in Pleasanton, California, walked home alone. She had recently stopped taking the school bus to avoid being teased, and her shortcut ran along a footpath and through a drainage culvert under Interstate 680. She was last seen alive at about 2:35 p.m. Around 3 p.m., fellow students found her. She had been killed. There were no witnesses, no weapon and no fingerprints that led anywhere, and the case went cold. For 27 years, her mother waited for an answer. But near the scene, police had collected Tina's purse, found hanging in a tree, and it carried a tiny trace of blood that was not hers. In 1984, no laboratory in the world could read it. In late 2007, Pleasanton police sent the old evidence to two laboratories for modern DNA testing. What came back in 2011 pointed to someone much closer to Tina than anyone had imagined.

In this documentary: who Tina was, the afternoon of April 5, 1984, the long cold years, how a trace of blood became a DNA profile, the arrest, the trial, the appeal and the confession that came 36 years later.
