TEXAS 2010 Cold Case Identified After 6 Years — The Woman Who Lived as Lori Ruff

On Christmas Eve, December 24, 2010, a mother in her early forties known as Lori Erica Ruff died by suicide in her vehicle in the driveway of her husband's parents' home in Longview, Texas, after her marriage ended that year. Days later, her family forced open a locked box from her closet. Inside was the birth certificate of Becky Sue Turner, a two-year-old girl who died in a house fire in Fife, Washington, in 1971, and court papers showing that in July 1988 a young woman using that name had changed it in Dallas to Lori Erica Kennedy. Before 1988, there was nothing. No parents, no childhood, no real name. A Social Security investigator in Seattle spent years on the file. The Seattle Times asked readers around the world for help. And a forensic genealogist turned to something the woman could not erase: the DNA she had passed on to her own daughter. Nearly six years after her death, a family on the other side of the country looked at her driver's license photo and said her name.

In this documentary: the life she built under a borrowed name, the toddler whose name was taken, the 27 days in 1988 that created a new identity, the investigator and the reporter who would not let it go, and how genetic genealogy finally gave her name back.
