MICHIGAN 1987 Cold Case Solved After 35 Years — Justice for Roxanne Wood

On the night of February 19, 1987, Roxanne Wood, 30, and her husband Terry spent the evening at a bowling alley near Niles, Michigan. In the early hours of February 20, Roxanne drove home alone, about 45 minutes ahead of him. When Terry walked into their home on Tam-O-Shanter Lane shortly after 1 a.m., he found her dead on the kitchen floor. There was no sign of forced entry. Terry had asked for a lawyer, and from that night on he was the man investigators looked at hardest. He was never charged, but for 35 years he lived as a person of interest in his own wife's murder. In 2013, he told a reporter just four words: "I didn't murder Roxanne." The killer had left behind a trace of DNA so small that an expert called it "a gnat's eyebrow." It took a team of Western Michigan University students, a private DNA lab, a genetic genealogist working for four days, Michigan State Police surveillance and one discarded cigarette butt to finally put a name to it.

In this documentary: who Roxanne Wood was, how her husband became the prime suspect, why the case stayed cold for 35 years, how genetic genealogy cracked an "unsolvable" sample, and how the case ended in a Berrien County courtroom.
