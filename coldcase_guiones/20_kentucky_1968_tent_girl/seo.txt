KENTUCKY 1968 Cold Case Identified After 30 Years — The Tent Girl Was Barbara Taylor

On May 17, 1968, a man named Wilbur Riddle was looking for old glass insulators along U.S. Route 25, just north of Georgetown, Kentucky, when he found a heavy bundle of green canvas, the kind used to make tents. Inside was the body of a young woman. She was about five feet one, with reddish-brown hair, and investigators guessed she was a teenager. No one reported her missing. No one claimed her. She was buried in the Georgetown Cemetery under a donated headstone with a police sketch and two words: Tent Girl. For 30 years, that was her only name. Then Riddle's son-in-law, Todd Matthews, who first heard the story as a 17-year-old on Halloween night in 1987, began a search that lasted a decade. In 1998, using the early internet, he found a post written by a woman in Arkansas who was still looking for her missing sister. An exhumation and a mitochondrial DNA comparison would finally give the Tent Girl back her name and reveal a story her family had never been told.

In this documentary: the life of the young mother behind the nickname, why the 1968 investigation stalled, the amateur internet search that cracked the case, the DNA confirmation, the suspect who was never charged, and how this case helped inspire the Doe Network and NamUs.
