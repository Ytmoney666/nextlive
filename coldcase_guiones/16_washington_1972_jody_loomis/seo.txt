WASHINGTON 1972 Cold Case Solved After 48 Years — Justice for Jody Loomis

On the afternoon of August 23, 1972, 20-year-old Jody Loomis set out on her bicycle from her family's home on Winesap Road, between Bothell and what is now Mill Creek, Washington, to ride her horse at a nearby stable. On her feet she wore a pair of waffle-soled boots, size five and a half, borrowed from her 12-year-old sister, Jana. She was last seen around 5 p.m. riding toward Penny Creek Road. About half an hour later, two target shooters found her in the woods. She had been sexually assaulted and shot in the head. For decades, the murder was Snohomish County's oldest cold case. In 2008, a crime lab found a microscopic stain on her left boot and built a DNA profile, but the national database had no match. It took 46 years, genetic genealogy, a family tree of six brothers and a coffee cup thrown away at a casino before detectives knocked on a door. And 48 years after that summer afternoon, the little sister who lent the boots saw them again in a courtroom.

In this documentary: who Jody Loomis was, the 30 minutes that ended her life, the boot that waited 36 years to be tested, how genetic genealogy found one family among millions, the 2020 trial, and the verdict that came hours too late for the man it named.
