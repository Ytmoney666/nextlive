CALIFORNIA 1985 Cold Case Solved After 25 Years — Justice for the Lost Women of South LA

On November 20, 1988, Enietra Washington was walking through South Los Angeles to a friend's house when a man in an orange Ford Pinto offered her a ride. She was shot in the chest, assaulted, photographed and pushed out of the car to die. She survived. Seven women in the same neighborhoods had already been killed since August 1985, starting with 29-year-old Debra Jackson, and their bodies had been left in alleys and trash bins. Then the killings seemed to stop, until a 15-year-old girl was found in 2002 and a 25-year-old woman on New Year's Day, 2007. For decades, the women of South LA were not warned that a serial killer was among them. Families were not told their daughters' cases were linked. A newspaper reporter broke the story in 2008 and gave the killer a name: the Grim Sleeper. The answer finally came in 2010, from California's new familial DNA program, a young man's unrelated weapons conviction, and an undercover officer posing as a busboy at a pizza restaurant.

In this documentary: who the ten women and the survivor were, how the community fought a slow police response, how familial DNA works, the undercover pizza-crust sample, the 2016 trial, and how the case ended in 2020.
