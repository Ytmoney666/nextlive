HAWAII 1991 Cold Case — DNA Identified the Killer After 32 Years | Dana Ireland

On Christmas Eve 1991, 23-year-old Dana Ireland, a recent George Mason University graduate from Virginia spending the holidays with her family, went out on her sister's bicycle in Kapoho, in the Puna district of Hawaii's Big Island. A vehicle struck her on Kapoho Kai Drive. She was taken about five miles away to a remote fishing trail off Waa Waa Road, where she was sexually assaulted, beaten and left behind. She was found alive, but it took more than two hours for an ambulance to reach her, and she died shortly after midnight on Christmas Day in a hospital in Hilo. Under enormous public pressure, police built a case on shifting statements and a jailhouse informant, and three local men were convicted. But the DNA on the evidence, including a T-shirt found near the trail, never matched any of them. It sat in storage for 32 years. Then the Hawaii Innocence Project, genetic genealogy and a discarded fork changed everything, with one final turn no one saw coming.

In this documentary: who Dana was, the investigation and trials that sent three men to prison, the fight to overturn two of those convictions, the genetic genealogy breakthrough, and why no court has ever heard the case against the man DNA identified.
