MINNESOTA 1993 Cold Case Solved After 25 Years — Justice for Jeanie Childs

On Sunday, June 13, 1993, a tenant at Horn Towers, an apartment building on Pillsbury Avenue South in Minneapolis, Minnesota, noticed water flowing out of the apartment of 35-year-old Jeanne Ann "Jeanie" Childs. A security guard went inside. The shower was still running. The bedroom window was wide open, its screen missing. Jeanie lay on the bedroom floor. She had been stabbed dozens of times. Beneath the window were bare footprints in blood. Jeanie was wearing socks, so the prints were not hers. She was a daughter, a sister and an aunt, but her murder barely made the news, and the case went cold. In 2015, Minneapolis police reopened it with the FBI and the state crime lab. A DNA profile and genetic genealogy pointed toward one Minnesota family. Then, in January 2019, investigators followed a man to a hockey game and waited for him to throw away a napkin. It took 25 years, but what that napkin held finally gave the footprints a name.

In this documentary: who Jeanie was, the scene investigators found, the long cold years, the genetic genealogy breakthrough, and the trial that followed.
