IOWA 1979 Cold Case Solved After 39 Years — Justice for Michelle Martinko

On the evening of December 19, 1979, 18-year-old Michelle Martinko, a senior at Kennedy High School in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, left her choir banquet at the downtown Sheraton Inn and drove alone to Westdale Mall, which had opened less than two weeks earlier. She was going to pick up a winter coat she had put on layaway. She was last seen around 8 p.m. near a jewelry store. At about 4 a.m. the next morning, police found her stabbed to death inside her family's 1972 Buick Electra in the mall parking lot. There was no weapon, no fingerprints and no witness. But Michelle had fought back, and her attacker had cut himself. His blood stayed on her dress and on the gearshift. In 2006 that blood produced a male DNA profile that never matched anyone in CODIS. For 39 years, the man it belonged to had no name. Then investigators turned to genetic genealogy, a distant cousin's upload to a public database, and a drinking straw left behind at a restaurant. What they found stunned a city that had never stopped asking who killed Michelle.

In this documentary: who Michelle was, the night she disappeared, the 39-year investigation, the genetic genealogy breakthrough, and the trial that followed.
