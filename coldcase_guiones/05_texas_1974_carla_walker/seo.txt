TEXAS 1974 Cold Case Solved After 46 Years — Justice for Carla Walker

In the early hours of Sunday, February 17, 1974, 17-year-old Carla Walker, a junior and cheerleader at Western Hills High School, was sitting with her boyfriend, Rodney McCoy, in his car in the Ridglea Bowl parking lot in Fort Worth, Texas, after their school's Valentine's dance. An armed man came to the car. He struck Rodney with a pistol that clicked three times without firing, knocked him unconscious, and took Carla. Three days later, on February 20, she was found in a culvert near Benbrook Lake. She had been strangled. Investigators had one solid clue: the magazine of a .22 caliber Ruger pistol, left in the parking lot. By early March 1974, detectives were working through a list of about two dozen buyers. Then the case went cold. For 46 years, Carla's brother Jim kept calling. In 2019, two detectives reopened the file, and a lab sequenced about four nanograms of DNA from Carla's clothing. What genetic genealogy found next had been sitting in the case file since 1974.
