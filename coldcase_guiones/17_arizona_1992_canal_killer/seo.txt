ARIZONA 1992 Cold Case Solved After 22 Years — Justice for Angela Brosso and Melanie Bernas

On the evening of November 8, 1992, Angela Brosso, 21, went out for a bike ride in north Phoenix, Arizona, on the eve of her 22nd birthday. Her boyfriend stayed home baking her birthday cake. She never came home, and she was found the next morning near the Arizona Canal. Ten months later, on September 21, 1993, 17-year-old high school junior Melanie Bernas went out on her own evening bike ride while her mother was at dinner. She was found in the same canal, about a mile and a half away. DNA showed that one man was responsible for both murders, but it matched no one. For 22 years, the Phoenix canal murders stayed cold. Then a forensic genealogist offered police something almost no one had tried: a family surname read from the killer's own DNA, found in public genealogy databases built by hobbyists. That lead, a detective's long list of names, and a lunch with undercover officers finally gave the case a name.

In this documentary: the lives of Angela and Melanie, the 22-year investigation, one of the first uses of genetic genealogy by U.S. police, and the trial that ended with a death sentence.
