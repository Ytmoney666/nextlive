CALIFORNIA 1975 Cold Case Solved After 43 Years — Justice for the Golden State Killer's Victims

On the evening of February 2, 1978, Air Force sergeant Brian Maggiore, 21, and his wife, Katie, 20, were walking their dog through their neighborhood in Rancho Cordova, near Sacramento, California. They were chased and shot. Their killer vanished. The Maggiores were two of 13 people murdered across California between 1975 and 1986 by one unknown offender who also attacked dozens more, from Visalia and Sacramento to Santa Barbara, Ventura and Orange County. The press gave him different names in different places: the Visalia Ransacker, the East Area Rapist, the Original Night Stalker. Police agencies worked apart, and the science of the time could not name him. Years later, DNA proved the crimes in the north and the south were the work of one man. The victims' families refused to give up, and writer Michelle McNamara gave him the name that stuck: the Golden State Killer. Then, in 2018, an investigator and a genealogist uploaded crime-scene DNA to a public genealogy website and began building family trees from more than a thousand distant cousins. After 43 years, the man behind the masks was about to have a name.

In this documentary: the victims and the families who fought for them, the decades-long manhunt, the genetic genealogy breakthrough, and the courtroom ending.
