WASHINGTON 1982 Cold Case Solved After 19 Years — Justice for the Green River Victims

On Sunday, August 15, 1982, a man rafting down the Green River near Kent, Washington, saw two bodies in the shallow water. Marcia Chapman was 31. Cynthia Hinds was 17. Nearby lay Opal Mills, 16, who had been seen at a phone booth three days earlier. A month before, 16-year-old Wendy Coffield had been found in the same river, and days earlier, 23-year-old Debra Bonner. Girls and young women kept disappearing from Pacific Highway South, the motel strip beside Sea-Tac Airport, many of them teenagers. A task force of 25 officers was formed, broken up, rebuilt, and finally reduced to a single detective who volunteered to keep going. A victim's family pointed police to a pickup truck in 1983. A suspect passed a polygraph in 1984. In 1987, detectives sealed a small piece of gauze. For 14 years it waited in storage, until a crime lab tried what one scientist called a last-ditch effort. This is the story of the Green River victims, the families who waited more than 40 years for answers, and the detectives and scientists who refused to let them be forgotten.

In this documentary: who the victims were, the 1982 discoveries, the long cold years and missed chances, the DNA match that broke the case, the plea and sentencing, and the genetic genealogy that named the last unknown victims in 2023 and 2024.
